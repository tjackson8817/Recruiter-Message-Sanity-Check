Email headers (optional — email only, not LinkedIn messages)
Headers show where an email really came from. To copy them: Gmail — open the email, ⋮ menu, Show original. Outlook on the web — ⋯ menu, View, View message details. Outlook desktop — File, Properties, Internet headers. Apple Mail — View, Message, All Headers. Paste everything above the message body.
Check the profile photo (optional, done on other sites)
Save the sender's profile photo first: open their LinkedIn profile, click the photo to enlarge it, then right-click and save it (on a phone, take a screenshot and crop it). Upload it to the sites below yourself. This tool never sends the photo anywhere.
Is the photo used elsewhere? (reverse image search)
Google Lens TinEye Bing Visual Search Yandex Images
Yandex is often the best at matching faces, but it's a Russia-based service. Skip it if that concerns you.
Is the photo AI-generated?
Is It AI? Quillbot AI Image Detector Hive AI-Generated Content Detection
Detectors give false results both ways, so also look for yourself: mismatched earrings or glasses, warped backgrounds, blurry hairlines, oddly smooth skin, and eyes centered in exactly the same spot as other AI headshots.     This tool checks for wording, link, domain, and email-header patterns common in fake recruiter messages and fake job postings. It does not verify anyone's real identity, employer, or listing. For that, find the company's real website yourself, check its careers page for a matching role, and be wary of any process that moves to WhatsApp/Telegram or asks for money, bank details, or ID documents before an offer. History lasts only for this browser session unless you download it. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is saved automatically: the page’s security policy blocks all outside requests, and its fonts are built in. Version 2.1.
